# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

F1 fans who want a credible, readable forecast for a race weekend without being data scientists. They come for a specific Grand Prix or sprint, check what the model expects and how sure it is, and leave.

The headline answer is plain and immediate. Depth is there on demand, and it is explained with the data, not asserted. One screen serves both the quick reader and the curious one.

## Product purpose

An independent F1 2026 prediction system. It forecasts qualifying and the race and updates after every session across the regulation reset. Launched around April 2026. The near-term goal is an audience: fans who come back each weekend because the forecasts have earned trust, with accuracy on the record all season.

## Positioning

The F1 forecast that knows how much to trust each signal. In a reset year the hard part is weighting a stale baseline, hidden testing pace and noisy Friday practice. Every screen should show that the system is disciplined about what it believes and why.

## Brand personality

Professional and engaging. Motorsport energy on the surface, evidence underneath. Numbers lead, the tone stays analytical, confident without hype. A serious analytics tool that happens to be about racing. It says what it knows and shows the uncertainty around it.

## Anti-references

- Not a betting site: no neon odds, no hype buttons, no sportsbook look.
- Not a generic SaaS dashboard: no cream and blue template, no identical metric tiles or card grids.
- Not official: no F1 or team liveries or logos.

## Design principles

1. **Show the work.** Calibration, accuracy tracking and uncertainty are the product's credibility. Make them easy to read, not hidden behind point numbers.
2. **Honest confidence.** Lead with intervals and probabilities. Uncertainty should read as rigor.
3. **Answer first, depth on demand.** A fan gets the answer at a glance. The evidence is one step away and explained with the data.
4. **Energy serves the data.** Motion and heat are accents. When energy and legibility conflict, legibility wins.
5. **Visibly independent.** Credible on its own, never borrowing official branding.

## Accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA: body text contrast at least 4.5:1 (large text 3:1), visible focus on every control, full keyboard use, and a reduced-motion version of every animation. Check the dark theme and the orange accent against these numbers; do not assume they pass.
